The 2026–27 event

El Niño, measured rather than assumed

NOAA's Oceanic Niño Index (ONI) is the three-month mean sea-surface temperature anomaly in the central Pacific. On 8 October 2026 the Climate Prediction Center declared an El Niño Advisory with the index at +2.1 °C and more than an 83 per cent chance of a strong to very strong event through early 2027. The chart on the left is the full index since 1950; the one on the right asks how much a given ONI value has historically pushed global temperature above its trend in the following year. That slope, not a guessed number, is what the correction above applies.

Local view

What it means for Chennai

The Permanent Service for Mean Sea Level holds the Chennai (Madras) tide-gauge record from 1916. Plotted against the same baseline as the satellite series, it shows how a global number becomes a local one: the long-term rise at the gauge is slower than the global mean, then the record jumps after 2017 in a way that the satellites do not see.

Method

How the projections are made

  1. Each series is the producing agency's own file, downloaded on 31 October 2026: NOAA GML (CO₂, CH₄), NASA GISS (temperature), NSIDC (sea ice), the University of Colorado Sea Level Research Group (global sea level, release 2026_rel2), NOAA NCEI (ocean heat), NOAA CPC (ONI) and PSMSL (Chennai). Seasonal cycles are removed where the agency provides a trend column.
  2. Two candidate models, linear and quadratic, are fitted by least squares over the chosen window. Both are first fitted on data to 2020 only and scored on 2021–2026 (the backtest panel). The quadratic is used only where it beats the linear model by at least 25 per cent in that hindcast (it does for CO₂ and methane, whose growth is accelerating); otherwise the linear model is kept, since a quadratic extrapolated beyond its data invents accelerations. The residual standard deviation of the chosen fit gives the ±1σ band.
  3. The El Niño correction is a regression of the annual temperature residual (observed minus quadratic trend, 1970–2025) on the October–March mean ONI of the preceding winter, and of the sea-level residual on the calendar-year mean ONI. The slopes are per unit ONI for temperature and per unit ONI for sea level. They are applied to 2027 using the assumed ONI peak chosen above and tapered to zero by 2028.
  4. Ice-sheet mass has no monthly series here; it is drawn at NASA's published GRACE/GRACE-FO rates (Greenland about −266 Gt a year, Antarctica about −135 Gt a year) and excluded from the backtest.

Limitations: a polynomial has no physics in it and will not foresee a volcanic eruption, an emissions policy or a change in ocean circulation; the ONI correction explains about half the year-to-year variance in temperature, not all of it; and the Chennai gauge carries datum and land-motion uncertainties that PSMSL itself flags. The controls exist so that a viewer can see how much each answer depends on these choices, which is the honest state of any two-year forecast.
